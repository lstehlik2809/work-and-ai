# Methodology and limitations

## Three search paths

The interface offers three independent ways to find an occupation, in this order:

1. **Job title:** look up official titles and sourced aliases, with completion and approximate spelling support.
2. **Find by skills:** select from 35 O*NET skills. Published importance and how much skills distinguish occupations rank 910 rated roles; 86 mapped roles without ratings are excluded. Unselected skills are unspecified. Matching retains the supporting O*NET role while presenting the corresponding BLS occupation and its statistical scope. This is not a personal-fit assessment or a measure of individual skills' AI exposure.
3. **Describe your work:** match responsibilities against reference descriptions and tasks using MiniLM local semantic inference with an immediate wording-only fallback. Opening this path clears the title; its suggestions are independent of the previous title and skill selection.

Each path requires occupation confirmation before displaying statistics and shares the Show top control (one to ten suggestions, default five). AI exposure and employment outlook never determine matching. The detailed retrieval rules and [skill-selection method](#skill-selection-matching-onet-310) below describe the evidence and limitations.

## Published information and interpretation

Work & AI retrieves existing occupation-level statistics after the user confirms a match. The app does not estimate individual job loss, skills, employability, suitability, readiness or training needs. Duties vary across employers, locations and people. These U.S. estimates are not automatically representative of another country.

The BLS August 27, 2026 release combines five external measures of relative AI exposure: three relate AI capabilities to work and two map observed interactions to occupational tasks/activities. Such interactions do not establish that people employed in an occupation used AI at work. BLS normalizes inputs and imputes some missing source values. The app preserves the published Low, Moderate, High and Very high categories without converting them to numerical distances, averaging them, or filling gaps.

The categories do not distinguish assistance from automation. They are not replacement probabilities, adoption forecasts, wage effects or demonstrated productivity benefits. The theoretical evidence dates from 2020 and 2023, no later than mid-2023; usage evidence is mainly 2024–2025. A 2026 publication date does not mean current capabilities were measured. Evidence is concentrated on language models and early users of particular products. See the [BLS method](https://www.bls.gov/emp/publications/ai-exposure-categories.htm) for the upstream research and caveats.

Employment growth is projected overall percent change over 2025–2035. It has multiple determinants and is not a causal estimate of AI's effect. Projections describe a long-term path under assumptions, not observed change or a range of possible outcomes. No occupation-level confidence interval is supplied here. Annual openings include replacement needs and growth, not just net new jobs. Their original thousands-of-jobs units are converted to jobs per year, preserving published rounding.

## Result context: category, evidence dates, and AI outlook notes

Each confirmed result includes an app-authored explanation of its published relative exposure group. Low, Moderate, High, and Very high remain ordered categories under the combined BLS measures; the app assigns no percentage, replacement risk, or numerical distance to them. A missing category is explicitly unavailable. The adjacent timeframe distinguishes the August 27, 2026 publication from theoretical capability evidence in 2020 and 2023 and observed-use evidence mainly in 2024–2025. It does not describe today's capabilities or establish worker adoption.

A separate optional reference uses [BLS Table 1.12, Factors affecting occupational utilization](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/factors-affecting-occupational-utilization.htm), for the 2025–2035 projections. These are assumptions about an occupation's share of industry staffing. A falling staffing share can coexist with growing total employment. The notes are neither the rationale for the AI exposure category nor an estimate of total job losses caused by AI. Each note preserves the full source wording, occupation and industry codes/titles, workbook row, projection period, and source link. Industry-specific rows retain their named industry; `Total, all industries` (`TE1000`) is labeled separately. Notes join by canonical BLS code, so specialist O*NET descriptions do not change the note's statistical scope.

From all 935 valid source rows, the app selects 56 notes covering 50 occupations that explicitly contain a word-bounded, case-insensitive `AI`, `artificial intelligence`, `machine learning`, or singular/plural `large language model` term. Phrase separators may be whitespace. Generic automation alone is excluded. All eligible rows are retained, including multiple notes for the same occupation. A successful lookup with no selected note means only that this explicit-term subset contains no note for the occupation; it does not establish no AI effect or comprehensive source coverage.

The workbook and full-table intermediate have separate SHA-256 pins in [data/ai-context-source-manifest.json](data/ai-context-source-manifest.json). Offline Python extraction reproduces the complete table; Node verifies those pins and deterministically creates `public/data/ai-context.json` and its runtime integrity pin. Ordinary CI checks exact artifact reproduction without Python, network access, or a new dependency. The browser loads the optional reference after occupation confirmation and verifies its bytes before showing notes. Loading and failure remain distinct from source absence, with retry; search, published figures and comparison do not depend on the optional reference. Context follows the current BLS code during selection changes and is removed when the result is cleared. Full notes, source scope and date caveats remain visible in single-result print output. CSV, comparisons, shared links, ranking and the existing occupational/semantic data are unchanged.

## Classification and coverage

BLS detailed National Employment Matrix results are canonical. Inclusion uses the directory's explicit Line item designation; 281 summary rows are excluded. The official BLS O*NET-SOC 2019/NEM crosswalk supplies all joins. No title similarity or decimal-suffix rule establishes a relationship. The snapshot contains 831 BLS results and 996 O*NET relationships. Each O*NET description and selected task retains its source identity.

The app flags broader scope when multiple O*NET roles share a result, or conservatively when published titles differ after normalization. This is a presentation warning, not a mapping inference. The figures apply to the whole BLS result and are not separate specialist estimates. The [exception report](data/exceptions.json) records 249 flagged relationships, 20 unmapped O*NET occupations (Legislators and 19 military roles), and 86 mapped roles without Core tasks. All current BLS results have verified exposure; missing-value paths are tested and preserve unavailable values. See [DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md) for full provenance and independent reconciliation.

## Lexical retrieval

Case, Unicode compatibility forms, whitespace, punctuation and ampersands are normalized. Sourced short titles supply abbreviations; ambiguous aliases retain all canonical relationships. Exact official/alias matches take priority. In the historical conservative API, more than three distinct results request specificity; the app's explicit result-limit mode uses a fixed ten-result ambiguity cutoff before displaying the requested number. Generic role terms still request specificity. Full-title plural variants are a secondary lookup. Fuse.js handles approximate spelling, with a development maximum score of 0.24 and a 0.055 best-match window. Assistant, technician, manager, nurse and engineer distinctions must not be silently added or removed by fuzzy matching.

BM25 uses one deduplicated document per canonical occupation, built from titles, descriptions and selected tasks. Parameters are k1=1.4 and b=0.75; common function/generic work words are excluded, and a simple trailing-s term normalization is used. Ordinary responsibility retrieval requires at least two supported informative terms (IDF >1.4), at least 0.40 coverage, and at least 0.82 of the leading qualified lexical score. These small-development-set rules are not universal suitability thresholds.

## Semantic index and hybrid retrieval

The production release retains the prior live MiniLM model, index and ranking. The MPNet experiment improved some specific-role leads but failed mixed-role release checks; it and the subsequent ranking redesign are deferred by user choice. Fresh independent validation was not completed. See the dated [experiment verification](verification/mpnet-implementation.md) and [scoped release verification](verification/live-improvements.md). Historical reports retain their original results.

The immutable encoder configuration is recorded in `src/semantic/config.json`: Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 at revision `751bff37182d3f1213fa05d7196b954e230abad9`, q8 ONNX, mean pooling, L2 normalization, 384 dimensions, 256 tokens. Transformers.js 4.2.0 uses stable ONNX Runtime Web 1.29.0, pinned through an npm override; offline CPU embedding uses ONNX Runtime 1.24.3. Browser vectors and metadata are checked before inference.

The exact 952 tested passage texts and 831 canonical associations are frozen in `data/semantic-passages.json`, with their original MiniLM construction identity and 256-token selection budget. The archived MPNet experiment verified their source, hashes and actual token counts without changing their content. Production profiles are constructed from the BLS title, O*NET title, whole description sentences and up to three selected Core tasks per role. The original builder retains only whole fragments that fit its tokenizer limit and records omitted fragments. It selects at most three evenly spaced, code-sorted, deduplicated roles per BLS result. All aliases remain in lexical retrieval rather than being stuffed into model inputs. This bounded sample cannot represent every specialist role.

Each passage is normalized. Their deduplicated mean is normalized again, producing exactly one vector per canonical occupation. Cosine retrieval therefore gives no extra maximum-score opportunities to occupations with more passages. Duplicate/ordering metamorphic tests and a first-passage ablation assess this choice; neither proves complete absence of coverage bias.

Hybrid search first computes the original grounded shortlist for every description. It combines the top 50 BM25 and semantic ranks using RRF, sum(1/(60+rank)), with ranks starting at one. A candidate needs cosine >=0.38, two informative lexical terms and >=0.35 lexical coverage. After fusion, alternatives must be within 0.055 cosine of the lead and retain >=0.82 of its lexical support score. For short responsibilities (at most 11 unique normalized terms), this is the final shortlist.

For longer descriptions, an existing grounded lead is retained unless a candidate with at least four informative overlaps and cosine >=0.55 exceeds its cosine by at least 0.10, and the original lead's lexical coverage is below 0.50. If no original candidate qualifies, the strongest semantic result with cosine >=0.38 and four informative overlaps supplies the initial bounded fallback. A longer query alone cannot displace an otherwise grounded lead.

When there is no original candidate, a bounded semantic fallback exists, and both that fallback and the global highest semantic score are below 0.55, the first BM25 result may instead lead if it has four informative overlaps, a top-five semantic rank and cosine >=0.35. This narrow path can recover a facet diluted in a mixed-role description. It also applies the supporting-role qualifier check below against the semantic fallback and the query; lexical rank alone cannot introduce a management or support level. Otherwise the initial bounded fallback remains. These cutoffs are provisional retrieval heuristics, not calibrated evidence of occupational fit.

Related alternatives are reconsidered only after an actual override or fallback, or when the retained primary is also the strongest four-overlap semantic candidate and exceeds the next such candidate by at least 0.10 cosine. Otherwise the entire original conservative shortlist is retained. Expanded alternatives independently need cosine >=0.38 and top-ten ranks in both semantic and BM25 retrieval. Ordinarily they need four informative overlaps and at least 0.75 of the lead's cosine. A secondary facet with three overlaps requires cosine at least as high as the grounded lead; such stronger semantic evidence takes priority among alternatives.

An expanded alternative's displayed supporting-role title cannot introduce assistant, technician, manager, supervisor, director, chief or head unless that qualifier occurs in the normalized/plural-adjusted query or the primary result's supporting-role title. The same check constrains the weak-semantic lexical fallback against the initial semantic fallback's supporting-role title. It preserves exact titles and original shortlists, and does not infer management responsibilities from general task overlap.

Within each expansion priority group, support from the defining source title and description determines order. That support sums IDF weights of unique shared terms and divides by the L2 norm of the source profile's IDF weights; the strongest role profile represents each canonical occupation. The historical conservative API retains only the first expanded alternative, offering at most two suggestions on the expansion path, or up to three in preserved original shortlists. Explicit result-limit calls retain up to the requested number using the same eligibility rules and order. These are heuristic retrieval rules, not evidence that other occupations cannot fit.

For these longer descriptions, the displayed specialty is selected from its full description and tasks, plus title overlap, using unique IDF-weighted terms. The excerpt is then selected within that specialty; titles and descriptions help prevent an incidental task from displaying the wrong specialist role. Ties use stable source identity ordering. The original source text, role, task ID when relevant, and source URL are retained. Generic attendance and answering verbs are excluded along with generic meetings and email terms so those alone request specificity.

The revised policy was developed on the user-supplied example and separately authored development descriptions, including paraphrases, expanded duties, single-focus occupations and abstention cases. Its first frozen implementation passed three of four independently withheld descriptions; one paraphrase omitted the psychology alternative because it had only three literal informative overlaps. That failure motivated the stronger-semantic-evidence exception above. A later broad audit exposed four previously correct leads lost by the general semantic-first policy (10/14 originally correct versus 6/14 for that experimental revision). This motivated preserving original grounding and restricting overrides and expansion. Four further fresh cases then exposed one mixed-role description whose data-science evidence fell below the semantic floor despite being the strongest lexical result; that failure informed the narrow weak-semantic fallback. These exposed sets now supply regression evidence, not untouched evaluations of the corrections; their first results remain retained. These limited examples are not representative-user validation. RRF, cosine, BM25 and profile-overlap scores are internal retrieval evidence, not calibrated probabilities. No exposure or outlook value enters ranking. That historical policy revision left the encoder, semantic vectors and source snapshots unchanged. The unpublished September 13 MPNet experiment changed the encoder and derived vectors with the same source passages; the inherited thresholds were not calibrated for it. The scoped production release preserves MiniLM and its original ranking unchanged.

The hybrid API preserves exact title/alias matches, including ambiguity, when a caller supplies a title. The current interface uses description search independently: opening its panel clears the title, and the app passes an empty title to the hybrid API. Previous title matches therefore do not constrain description suggestions. A confirmed selection is never silently replaced. Canonical suggestions carry sourced descriptions or task excerpts for confirmation. Unsupported input receives clarification or no match, never invented duties or nearest-neighbor statistics. Long descriptions are explicitly rejected rather than silently truncated.

The app defaults to showing up to five matching occupations, with a selectable limit from one to ten. It retrieves an eligible pool of up to ten once per search and changes the displayed prefix immediately without rerunning semantic inference. Fewer matches are shown whenever evidence or ambiguity rules require it; the list is never padded. The title, lexical and hybrid APIs accept an optional result limit, truncated and clamped to 1–10 (non-finite values use five). Calls that omit it preserve the historical conservative outputs, including the archived evaluation replays. Those historical top-three results do not evaluate the expanded app list.

The architecture was informed by the owner's [second-brain repository](https://github.com/lstehlik2809/people-analytics-second-brain) at commit `57348be59b6575fc5b329650ad54dc01f87e536b`: `pipeline/build_semantic_index.mjs`, `pipeline/static/hybrid-search.mjs`, and `pipeline/static/semantic-search.html`. The implementation is original; it does not copy source code or blog content. Its unconditional semantic shortlist, character chunks, development runtime and unverified cache claims were not adopted.

Labels were checked against source relationships but have no independent representative-user or subject-matter-expert review. They are a small provisional benchmark, not scientific validation. Fixture correction before freezing is recorded; three development runs preceded the original configuration freeze. The historical held-out evaluation ran once before the later matching revision described above. Its recorded results remain unchanged below; they are historical evidence rather than a fresh evaluation of the revised policy.

All ten held-out title cases had acceptable hybrid top-1 results. Responsibilities achieved only 2/4 top-1 and 3/4 top-3. These results do not establish broad superiority or a measured one-minute usability outcome.

## Skill-selection matching (O*NET 31.0)

This path uses the 10 Essential Skills and 25 Transferable Skills in the [O*NET 31.0 database](https://www.onetcenter.org/database.html), with definitions and group names from Content Model Reference. The [Essential Skills dictionary](https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/31.0/excel/essential_skills.html) and [Transferable Skills dictionary](https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/31.0/excel/transferable_skills.html) define the source columns. Acquired raw CSV bytes and compressed bytes are SHA-256 pinned separately in `data/skills-source-manifest.json`, with URL, timestamp, size, release, and CC BY 4.0 license. `scripts/data/skills.mjs` parses quoted CSV, validates source scales and row populations, joins by Element ID, and emits deterministic `public/data/skills.json`. This selected, combined representation is an app modification; USDOL/ETA has not endorsed it.

The sources contain 18,200 Essential and 45,500 Transferable rows: 35 skills × 910 roles × two scales. Ranking uses Importance (IM, 1–5); Level (LV, 0–7) is not interpreted as user proficiency. An IM suppression flag or Not Relevant flag on either scale removes that pair from match evidence. In this release 3,515 pairs are excluded because LV is Not Relevant; missing values remain null, not zero. The generated coverage report lists all 86 mapped O*NET roles without ratings; these roles are excluded from skills search and remain accessible through the other paths. There are 910 rated roles among the 996 mapped roles.

For each skill, the differentiating weight is `1 + ln((known ratings + 1) / (known ratings >= 3 + 1))`. O*NET importance 3 is Important. A role's score is the weighted sum of `(importance - 1) / 4` for selected, available ratings, divided by the total weight of selected skills. A missing rating contributes no positive evidence; keeping its selected weight in the denominator avoids rewarding missing data. This is an evidence-coverage penalty, not an imputed zero skill rating. Unselected skills are unspecified and never counted against the user. Candidates need at least one selected skill rated 3 or higher. There is no personal-fit cutoff calibrated from outcomes; this is exploratory suggestion ranking. Ties use O*NET code. Rank individual O*NET roles first, then map and deduplicate BLS occupations while preserving the strongest matched role. The app displays source importance values (including that role's available selected skills rated at least 3) and flags unavailable selected ratings. AI exposure and employment outlook never enter matching.

Common skills remain useful but receive less distinguishing weight. The score is not exposed as a fit percentage, statistical probability, proficiency estimate, recommendation to retrain, or assessment of the user. Source-grounded sanity tests cover mechanical, programming, and teaching combinations and broad-to-specific changes; they do not establish general career-guidance validity. Synthetic tests cover missing ratings, invalid/duplicate selections, unselected skill invariance, stable prefixes, mapping deduplication, source flags and malformed payloads. Search runs locally after the small skills artifact is loaded on opening its panel; no semantic/model assets are needed. The initial static request contains no selected skills. Share links and CSV retain the existing public-occupation-only contract.
## Occupation map

The map is an exploratory view of relative AI exposure and shared occupational skills. The existing BLS exposure categories are not converted into probabilities, percentages of jobs lost, or a forecast of AI-caused employment change. Each occupation counts once: neither point size nor the category counts are weighted by employment, openings, or projected growth.

**Coverage and aggregation.** A point represents one canonical BLS occupation. Its distinct mapped O*NET role codes are deduplicated. For each of the 35 published skills, the map takes the arithmetic mean of available importance ratings across those roles. Null or absent ratings are excluded from that mean, not replaced with zero. A mean of at least 3 on O*NET's 1–5 importance scale defines the occupation's observed important-skill set. This is an aggregate description of mapped roles; it does not imply that every specialty requires each included skill.

The map requires at least 20 known mean ratings: 771 of 831 occupations qualify. Of the 60 excluded occupations, 59 have no ratings and Models (41-9012) has 19. The general profiles used by Skills patterns remain at 772 occupations with any ratings; their eligibility and threshold statistics are unchanged. Per-occupation details show rated skills, rated versus mapped roles and unavailable ratings. Coverage counts include both the full snapshot and mapped subset.

**One continuous comparison.** Both map geometry and exact connection rankings use the mean absolute difference of importance ratings, divided by four (the range of the 1–5 scale). Only skills rated for both occupations enter the mean; at least 20 jointly rated skills are required. Similarity is one minus that distance. Missing ratings remain null and are never imputed or treated as zero. Ratings on either side of the important-skill threshold still affect similarity. The threshold of 3 defines skill highlighting, important-skill counts and shared-important-skill labels, and is also used by Skill patterns, independently of this comparison. Displayed means and paired differences are rounded to two decimal places; similarity uses unrounded values. The comparison gives each jointly rated skill equal weight and treats equal steps on the importance scale as comparable. Agreement on low ratings also raises similarity. Because pairs can use different observed skill subsets, these dissimilarities need not satisfy the triangle inequality.

**Positions and missing support.** UMAP is fitted offline to a sparse precomputed distance matrix. Unsupported pairs are absent; supported zero distances and the diagonal are explicitly retained. Among the 771 eligible occupations, 15 pairs have insufficient joint evidence. Every occupation has at least 757 supported nonself neighbors. No exposure, growth, openings, embeddings or employment weights enter the projection or ranking. The selected configuration uses 15 neighbors, minimum distance 0.3, two dimensions, seed 11, 500 epochs, spectral initialization and one worker.

Coordinates are normalized isotropically, centered at 0.5 with a longest-axis span of 0.8, and rounded to eight decimals. The exact runtime TypeScript spacing function is then applied without further rounding. That same final geometry is scored offline. The browser validates schema 2, exact artifact bytes, finite bounded coordinates, complete unique code coverage and a fingerprint containing the release, skill IDs, numeric/null profiles and aggregation, support, eligibility, normalization and missing-data policy. A loading or validation failure shows retry; it never substitutes another projection.

**Frozen evaluation.** The protocol was written before fitting: neighbor counts 15, 30, 60 × minimum distances 0.1, 0.3, 0.5 × seeds 11, 29, 47 (27 runs). The primary measure is tie-aware recall at 5, 10 and 20 neighbors, averaged equally over occupations and neighborhood sizes. Strictly closer source neighbors must be retained; source ties at the kth boundary fill only the remaining quota. Source neighbors exclude unsupported pairs and self. Visual top-k is unrestricted, so any visually adjacent unsupported pair reduces recall. Visual distance ties resolve by canonical occupation code.

The old binary UMAP is evaluated against these same continuous distances on the common 771 occupations. Its original full 772-node spacing is computed first, then restricted without respacing. Its displayed recall is 10.35%, 16.56%, 25.16% (17.36% overall). Every configuration must have three successful seeds, mean overall recall at least 2 percentage points above baseline, mean recall no lower at any k and every seed's overall score above baseline. Selection uses highest qualifying mean, then worst seed, then prescribed grid order; the shipped seed is always 11. If none qualify, promotion stops without a fallback or further tuning.

All 27 runs succeeded and all nine configurations qualified. The selected three-seed mean is 39.49%, and the selected seed scores 39.20% overall. See the [continuous evaluation report](data/continuous-projection-evaluation/README.md) for per-k scores, every saved run, supplementary raw-coordinate recall and seed stability, exact reproduction and provenance. These are descriptive in-sample comparisons, not predictive accuracy or external occupational ground truth. Axes, global distances and apparent cluster gaps have no standalone meaning.

**Visual spacing.** The unchanged deterministic rule uses 120 collision-relaxation passes, a 10 SVG-unit separation target and maximum movement of 12 units (0.02 normalized), independent of exposure. It acts on the full eligible population once. Search, exposure filters and zoom never recompute it. Selected circles, highlights, camera framing and edges use the resulting display positions, while exact similarity uses the original ratings. On the selected continuous layout, intersecting pairs (circle radii only) fall from 1,496 to 997; the historical displayed binary map had 1,634. Some overlap remains. Scoring after spacing incorporates its neighborhood-fidelity cost.

**Historical evidence.** The original binary Jaccard/PCA experiment and spacing diagnostics remain unchanged in [the historical report](data/projection-evaluation/README.md). Their execution revision is `1946660c5258b69d465d2ec7d9161f5bdbc50b10`. Reproduce them in a separate checkout; the historical evaluator refuses to overwrite the active schema-2 artifact. The continuous evidence folder also retains the exact old artifact and integrity pin used for its baseline.

**Skill search.** Highlight on map offers suggestions labeled Skill or Occupation, matched by skill name or occupation title/code. Choosing one skill highlights occupations where its mean observed O*NET importance is ≥ 3/5, using the same profiles as the map and Skill patterns tab. Missing and below-threshold ratings do not match; this does not establish that the skill is absent. Exposure filters constrain highlighted occupations and the matching list. The status names the skill and reports its occupation count within those filters. Suggestions use all mapped occupations and all 35 skills so a choice can have zero matches within the current filters. Occupation suggestions show up to six titles; typing a narrower query refines them. Editing or clearing the query removes the prior skill choice and restores title/code matching. One skill can be selected at a time. Search highlights and frames matches without changing map positions, exposure encoding or the selected occupation's details.

**Connections.** Selecting an occupation ranks every supported nonself pair using the same continuous similarity as the layout. Up to five matches are ordered by descending similarity, then jointly rated skill count, then occupation code. The panel shows joint coverage, similarity and paired ratings; shared important-skill names are supplementary. Similarity is not AI exposure or a likelihood of a job match. Lines connect visible endpoints only; ranking always uses all eligible occupations. Selecting a hidden neighbor clears filters to reveal it. Approximate visual neighbors can differ from the exact ranking.

**Encoding and interaction.** Circle radii are four discrete ordered values for Low, Moderate, High and Very high exposure; they are category symbols, not a continuous or ratio scale. Color reinforces the category, with a separate hollow/dashed symbol for unavailable exposure. Exposure toggles control point visibility and support any combination of Low, Moderate, High, Very high and Unavailable. All is the initial state. Clicking a category from All starts a custom subset; subsequent toggles add or remove categories. Clicking All, removing the last selected category, or selecting all five restores All. The chart draws no guide axes, retaining only its frame and selected similarity connections. Text search highlights matching titles/codes with dark rings and draws them above contextual points; it narrows the occupation list while retaining other map points and their exposure colors. Empty queries add no highlights; an unsuccessful search reports zero highlights without removing contextual points. Search does not automatically select an occupation. Neither interaction recomputes positions or coverage denominators. The occupation list orders exposure categories from highest to lowest, uses alphabetical ordering within categories, and makes occupations accessible even when circles overlap.

Selecting through a node, list entry, neighbor or explicit keyboard action centers the camera on that occupation at 2.5× zoom, keeping its surroundings visible. After 200 milliseconds without another search change, a single highlighted result receives the same framing; multiple results are framed together with padding at no more than 2.5×, without selecting one. A cleared query or zero matches leaves the camera unchanged. Query/exposure changes cancel stale search framing, and selection or manual camera actions cancel any pending search movement. These changes affect the viewport only, never canonical point coordinates.

Mouse-wheel zoom is anchored at the pointer and bounded from 1× to 4×; scrolling outside the graph and Ctrl+wheel browser zoom retain their normal behavior. Keyboard users can focus the map and press +/− to zoom; **Reset view** restores the initial viewport even with an unchanged active query. Background dragging and pan buttons move a zoomed view. Double-clicking the background, pressing Escape on the map or a node, or using **Clear selection** removes selection and its connection lines while preserving the camera, query, search highlights and exposure filter. Double-clicking a node selects it; automatic camera movement between clicks does not turn that gesture into background deselection. A visible explanation next to the graph describes the rating-similarity connections and distinguishes skill similarity from equal exposure or causality. Details connect to the existing occupation view. The map runs locally from the already validated skills artifact and does not download a model or send input to another service.

## Skill patterns by AI exposure category

The Skill patterns tab describes **skill profiles of occupations grouped by estimated AI exposure**. It compares how often each of the 35 O*NET skills is important in a selected BLS AI exposure category with **all eligible occupations, including the selected category**, by default. A comparison selector also offers **rest: all three other known categories combined**. Very high and overall are the defaults. This describes associations in occupational requirements; it neither assigns AI exposure to skills nor estimates individual risk, employability or retraining needs. Counts are occupations, unweighted by employment, growth or openings.

**Relationship to the exposure measures.** The [BLS exposure categories](https://www.bls.gov/emp/publications/ai-exposure-categories.htm) combine five measures based on O*NET abilities, tasks and work activities, including observed AI interactions mapped to tasks or activities. These inputs differ from the 35 O*NET skill-importance ratings compared here, but describe related aspects of occupational work. Some associations may therefore reflect how exposure was constructed. This is an additional descriptive comparison, not independent validation of the exposure categories or an independent measure of individual skills' AI exposure. It does not establish that a skill causes or protects against AI exposure.

**Observation and missingness.** The analysis reuses `buildOccupationProfiles`, the map's aggregation without projection: one BLS occupation per observation, arithmetic mean of available importance ratings across unique mapped O*NET roles, important at mean ≥ 3 on the 1–5 scale. Missing, suppressed and not-relevant ratings remain null and never enter as zero or below threshold. A mean describes the mapped roles jointly, not every individual specialty. Unknown exposure excludes an occupation from both groups. Known-exposure occupations with no ratings are reported separately; occupations with available ratings below 3 remain eligible. Every skill uses its own nonmissing denominators in each group. In the current snapshot, 772 of 831 occupations have known exposure and any skill rating; 59 have no ratings and none have unknown exposure. Per-skill coverage can be lower and systematically different between categories.

**Descriptive effects.** For a skill and category, the disjoint 2×2 table remains `[[a,b],[c,d]]`: selected-category important/below 3, then rest important/below 3. Selected prevalence is `a/(a+b)`, rest prevalence is `c/(c+d)`, and overall prevalence is `(a+c)/N`, where `N=a+b+c+d`. Overall includes selected and uses this skill’s nonmissing ratings, with equal weight per occupation. The prevalence ratio divides selected by the active baseline; the percentage-point difference is 100 times selected minus baseline. Counts, columns, explanations, highlights and numeric sorting use that baseline. All 35 skills initially rank by descending absolute percentage-point difference, preserving the sign and using skill-ID tie order; every column can be sorted in either direction, with undefined values last. Zero selected prevalence with positive baseline prevalence yields 0; positive selected prevalence with zero baseline prevalence yields infinity; zero in both yields an undefined ratio with equal-prevalence direction. ### Reproducible analysis and robustness

`npm run patterns:prepare` generates `public/data/skill-patterns.json` from the pinned occupation and skill sources; `npm run validate:patterns` requires exact regeneration of both the file and its pin. The schema-2 artifact contains descriptive counts and parameters under `occupational-skill-patterns-descriptive-v2`. The browser verifies artifact bytes, release, schema, generation identity and the actual loaded source objects against SHA-256 pins before enabling analysis or exports. A failed load is isolated to the pattern view and offers a retry.

The table and primary download use a fixed mean importance threshold of 3 out of 5, inclusive; there is no threshold selector. Sensitivity analysis retains thresholds of 2.5, 3 and 3.5. Twenty-seven fixed descriptive variants comprise those three thresholds, maximum available specialty importance at 3, restriction to at least 28 of 35 observed skills, and omission of each of 22 two-digit occupational families. All variants preserve missing values. Eligibility includes every known-exposure occupation in the declared population; only each skill's rated denominator excludes missing ratings. The coverage restriction includes 679 occupations. Maximum aggregation means any mapped specialty, not the typical occupation. Each omission reports the remaining and omitted populations.

Sparse counts mean fewer than 30 rated occupations or fewer than 10 important observations in either disjoint group. Direction changes require a difference strictly below −1 and strictly above +1 pp across available variants; magnitude sensitivity requires a range of at least 5 pp. These boundaries use exact rational comparisons of integer counts. Changes in membership of the five largest absolute differences are reported separately. Unavailable variants do not create changes. The range is descriptive, not a confidence interval, and these display rules are not significance tests.

The main analytical download contains all 35 skills for the selected category and baseline at the fixed threshold of 3, ordered by absolute difference, regardless of the private text filter or table sort. It includes counts, rated and eligible denominators, observed effects, robustness flags, source hashes and the descriptive method identity. A companion download contains all 27 variants, including alternative thresholds. The active exposure category and baseline are exported; private search text and Find by skills selections are not. The reading brief exports confirmed public occupations with dates, scope, source links, definitions and questions for practical investigation.

The map now uses the continuous comparison for both positions and connections, with no method selector. The numeric/null fingerprint and map-only eligibility do not change the Skills patterns aggregation or source artifacts.

The local improvement checks compare all 3,780 variant rows with a separately authored source-count calculation. Timing includes artifact parsing, integrity/source/schema validation, all 140 primary rows and four-category sensitivity calculations. Exact final timings and browser limitations are recorded in the improvement verification report. These checks are not representative-user, occupational-expert, screen-reader or physical-phone studies.

### Occupation downloads and shared links

CSV and public-link actions use the current comparison when it contains occupations; otherwise they use the confirmed occupation. The occupation panel states this scope. Its reading brief and PDF always use the confirmed occupation, while comparison briefs and PDFs use the comparison. Shared links retain only public BLS codes and the release identifier, not a selected O*NET specialty or private inputs. On opening a shared link, the app restores those occupations as a comparison and opens its first occupation with the first mapped role description.

### Printable PDF downloads

The occupation and comparison actions generate downloadable PDFs in the browser from the explicitly selected public records. The occupation PDF contains only the current occupation and selected role description; the comparison PDF contains the comparison occupations. Each includes published metrics, scope, interpretation, sources and release information. These are printable summaries; optional industry outlook notes and detailed skill lists remain in the app. PDF generation loads on demand and does not require a browser print dialog or send search text to a server.
